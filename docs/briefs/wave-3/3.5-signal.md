## Step one: the signal-cli client

Start and supervise `signal-cli -a <account> daemon --http 127.0.0.1:<port>` as a child in its own process group, restarting it with backoff when it dies (exact process id, never a name pattern); wait for its health endpoint; read events over its server-sent-events endpoint with reconnect at two to sixty seconds and a forced reconnect after two minutes of silence; send over its JSON-RPC endpoint, with a typing indicator while a reply is being written; download attachments into a cache under the home's Signal folder capped at two hundred megabytes, oldest removed first. Every outbound text goes through `contract.Secrets.Redact`.

## Step two: linking and pairing

`nerdgenie signal link` runs `signal-cli link -n nerdgenie`, reads the `sgnl://` link it prints, renders it as a QR code in the terminal through `github.com/mdp/qrterminal/v3` (pre-approved), waits for the phone to scan with a deadline, confirms with the account it was linked as, and writes the account into the configuration through a function `serve.go` supplies. Pairing: an unknown sender gets a message with an eight-character code from a thirty-two-character alphabet with no look-alike letters, valid one hour, at most three pending at once, at most one request per sender per ten minutes, lockout after five wrong tries; codes are stored salted and hashed in a file under the Signal folder and compared in constant time; approved senders are stored by their Signal identifier in the same folder; `/pair <code>`, a `contract.Command` this package exports and the orchestrator registers, approves the sender whose code matches, and works from the terminal only until one sender is paired.

## Step three: the channel

A `contract.Channel` named `signal`: receive (inbound messages from paired senders only; unpaired ones get the pairing message and nothing else), send (one message per reply when it fits Signal's limit, otherwise split at paragraph breaks into as few messages as possible, never mid-sentence), send a file, show a preview (the preview as the actual text with the answers `approve`, `always`, and `deny` explained once, waiting for the reply with the deadline), ask a secret (returns `contract.ErrNoMaskedPrompt` always), and health (the daemon's health endpoint). A "still working" note is sent once after five idle minutes during a task. A handoff arrives with the screenshot as an attachment. Inbound photos and files are saved into the home's inbox folder and their paths ride on the inbound message.

## Bounds

The attachment cache cap; the pending-code cap; the reconnect backoff cap; the split count cap; a message past a length cap is truncated with a note.

## Tests, in the order you write them

Against the fake signal-cli daemon and program in `testkit`, on the fake clock:

1. A message in and a reply out, with the typing indicator recorded.
2. A dropped stream reconnects with backoff; a stalled stream reconnects after two minutes of silence.
3. An attachment is downloaded into the cache and the cache cap evicts the oldest.
4. A long reply is split at paragraph breaks into the fewest messages, never mid-sentence.
5. The link flow against the fake program: the QR is rendered, the scan is awaited, the account is confirmed and handed to the configuration function; a timeout says so.
6. Every pairing rule, one test each: the code shape, the expiry, three pending, one request per ten minutes, lockout after five, constant-time compare, salted and hashed storage, `/pair` approves, an unpaired sender gets only the pairing message.
7. The channel contract check from `testkit` passes.
8. An inbound photo lands in the inbox and its path is on the message.
9. A preview round-trip with `approve`, `always`, `deny`, and a timeout; the "still working" note after five idle minutes, once.
10. Fuzz tests on the event decoder and the pairing-code parser: any bytes, no panic.
