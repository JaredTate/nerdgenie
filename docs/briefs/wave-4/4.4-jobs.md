## What to build

- **Job records.** A job is a record of kind job kept through `record`'s job rules: the goal, the rules, the task list with done, running, waiting, and scheduled entries and a date on the waiting ones, the reports with ids like `j4.2` and their full text in the log, the lessons. State is rebuilt from the log at start by replaying the job's events, and every change appends an event, so the log stays the truth.
- **The next-task rule.** `NextTask` returns the first unfinished, unclaimed task of the oldest running job whose due time has passed, claiming it with an atomic update in the database so two processes cannot claim one task; a task claimed but not finished within the task budget is released with a failure. `FinishTask` writes the report, marks the task done or failed, counts failures in a row (three pause the job with a message; ten switch a scheduled job off with a message), and closes a job whose last task is done. `Load` returns the record for the job summary and `/jobs 4`.
- **The scheduler.** For jobs with a schedule: `at`, `every`, or `cron` (through `github.com/robfig/cron/v3`, pre-approved) in the job's timezone; one timer clamped to sixty seconds; on each tick that is due, one task from the template, unattended; backoff of thirty seconds to sixty minutes after a failed tick; one incident per distinct error text, reported once; a sixteen-kilobyte notepad per job that a task may read and append to through the job record's lessons; a monitor mode where the template's task hashes its output and the model is woken only when the hash changes. A task can never create a job that restarts the agent (a fixed list of refused templates and commands).
- **`/jobs` and `/cron`.** Two `contract.Command` values: `/jobs` lists every job with its name, its progress ("3 of 12 tasks done"), and what it does in one line; `/jobs 4` prints the record. `/cron` lists the jobs that have a schedule, each with its name, its schedule in plain words ("every weekday at 7 in the morning", "every 2 hours", "once on 2026-10-01 at 09:00"), what it does in one line, when it last ran, and when it runs next; `/cron 3` shows one, `/cron run 3` runs it now, `/cron off 3` switches it off.
- **Bounds.** Tasks per job, jobs, the notepad, the incident list, the backoff cap, the timer clamp.

## Tests, in the order you write them

1. A job record round-trips through `record` and survives a restart by replay.
2. A task's report lands in the job with the right id and the next task is handed out; a task with a date waits for it on the fake clock.
3. A job with a schedule creates one task per tick for each of the three schedule kinds, in a named timezone.
4. Two processes cannot claim one task (integration, with a helper process on the real database).
5. Three failures pause the job and report; ten switch a scheduled job off and report; a claimed task past the budget is released with a failure.
6. An unattended task that hits the ask-me-first list stops and reports (through the loop's contract: the `Unattended` flag on the task).
7. Backoff, incidents, the notepad cap, and the monitor mode's hash rule, each on the fake clock.
8. The `/jobs` and `/cron` listings for three fixture jobs are golden files, and every schedule shape prints in plain words.
9. The contract check from `testkit` passes; the integration test runs on the real database in a temporary home.
10. A fuzz test on the schedule parser and the plain-words printer: any input, no panic.
