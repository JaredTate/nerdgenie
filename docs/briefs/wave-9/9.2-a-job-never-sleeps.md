## Part one: a stuck task goes to the back of the line

**What is wrong.** When the guard stops a job's task, the job picks it up once itself (`PickUpOnce`), and on the second stop `finishJobTask` puts the task down and the job waits for a person. On the flight-simulator night that wait was nine hours, three times, with twelve tasks that needed nothing from the stuck one.

**What to build.** `contract.Job` gains `Defer(ctx, jobID, taskID) (deferred bool, err error)`, with a doc comment: the first call marks the task deferred in the job's own state, the way `PickUpOnce` keeps its once, and answers true; a second call for the same task answers false. `NextTask` passes over a deferred task while any other task of the job is still unfinished and not deferred, and takes it again once only deferred tasks remain, in their order. Nothing in the record's task list changes; the job's own state holds the mark, so the record's rules on task order stand. In `finishJobTask`, the branch that today puts a guard-stopped task down first asks `Defer`: when it answers true, the person is told in one line, `Task t6 is set aside after stopping twice; job 2 goes on with the next task and comes back to t6 before its last task`, the task's record is kept as a put-down task is kept, and the loop goes on to the job's next task the way it does after a finished one; when it answers false, the task is put down for the person as now. The fake job store in `internal/testkit` does the same, and `CheckJob` proves the once on both stores.

**Tests first.** `internal/job`: `TestASecondGuardStopDefersTheTaskAndNextTaskPassesOverIt`, `TestADeferredTaskIsTakenAgainWhenOnlyDeferredTasksRemain`, `TestATaskDeferredTwiceIsNotDeferredAgain`. `internal/testkit`: the fake's `Defer` and the contract check. `internal/loop/jobpickup_test.go` or a new file: `TestASecondGuardStopSetsTheTaskAsideAndTheJobGoesOn`, `TestATaskSetAsideTwiceWaitsForThePerson`, on the fixture job with three tasks and the scripted fake model, the way the existing pick-up tests drive it.

## Part two: a task is told its cost against the job, and closed honestly

**What is wrong.** The sky task ran 443 rounds and ten hours against neighbours of 84 to 173 rounds, almost all of it on one done line, and nothing in the harness weighed one task against the job. The work order's own visual QA and final regression tasks exist to catch what earlier tasks left, and never got the chance.

**What to build.** A new `internal/loop/pace.go`. Two constants at its top with a sentence each: `CostLineAtTimesTheMedian = 2` and `CloseAtTimesTheMedian = 3`. The median is of the running time of the job's finished tasks, from `contract.Job.Timing` (a task's `Finished` less its `Started`, over tasks that have both), and there is no median, and no pacing at all, until two tasks have finished; a plain task outside a job is never paced. The current task's running time is measured from the start of this run of it. Once a round, at the hook in `run.go`, the task is measured. At twice the median the model reads one line, once: `this task has run 250 minutes against a median of 90 for this job's finished tasks; 3 of 4 done lines are proved; prove the open lines with what you hold, or write what stands in their way as a failure and close the task`. At three times the median the harness ends the task as failed with the report `paced out at three times the job's median: N of M done lines proved; not proved: <each open line's text>`, so the job's `FinishTask` takes it as a failed task and goes on, the job's later tasks read the report, and the job's own rule on three failures in a row stands. The done check is not bypassed: a task that has not proved a line does not close as done.

**Tests first, in `internal/loop/pace_test.go`** on the scripted fake model, the fake job store and the fake clock: `TestATaskAtTwiceTheJobsMedianReadsItsCostLineOnce`, `TestATaskAtThriceTheJobsMedianEndsFailedWithItsOpenLinesOnTheReport`, `TestAJobWithFewerThanTwoFinishedTasksPacesNothing`, `TestAPlainTaskOutsideAJobIsNeverPaced`.
